# Common App Aggregate Data Trends

> **What this is:** Real, primary-sourced platform-level data from Common App's own Data Analytics and Research (DAR) team — application volume, socioeconomic/first-gen trends, race/ethnicity trends, geography, and international applicant trends, aggregated across all 1,146 member institutions. Published as periodic "Deadline Update" briefs (~monthly, Nov–March) and an annual "End-of-Season Report" (most recent: 2025–2026 season, published Aug 20, 2026, at `commonapp.org/reports/end-season-report-2025-2026-first-year-application-trends/`). All figures below came directly from Common App's own PDFs and support pages, not secondary reporting, except where explicitly flagged otherwise.
>
> **Why this belongs in this project:** every admit-rate and CDS figure elsewhere in this archive is one school's number. This is the one source that shows the **applicant pool itself changing** — who's applying, from where, how many schools each student applies to — a different lens than any single school's data can provide, since it's the actual pipeline feeding every school's admit-rate calculation.

---

## 1. Structural facts

- **1,146 institutions** received applications in 2025–26 (up 4% from 1,097 in 2024–25; up 63.5% from 701 in 2016–17). Public members: 363 (up from 330, +10%, and +210% over the decade from 117); private: 714 (up 1% from 708, +40.6% over the decade).
- States with a public flagship on Common App rose from 21 (2016–17) to **48 of 50** (2025–26). In **11 states**, students can now apply to *any* public four-year university via Common App.
- **The University of California and Cal State systems do not use Common App** — both run their own separate application platforms (relevant to reading every UC figure elsewhere in this project — UC applicant volume is never captured in Common App's numbers).
- **Fee waiver eligibility criteria** (Common App's own definition): family receives public assistance; enrolled in a federal/state/local low-income aid program (GEAR UP, TRIO/Upward Bound, etc.); lives in subsidized public housing, foster care, or is homeless; SAT/ACT fee-waiver eligible; family income within USDA Food and Nutrition Service guidelines; ward of the state/orphan; Pell-eligible or a Pell recipient; or a supporting statement from a school official/counselor/community leader. **First-generation students are automatically eligible.**
- Common App does **not** collect household income directly — it uses fee-waiver self-report and ZIP-code-level Census median income (2019–2023 ACS 5-year estimates) as socioeconomic proxies, an important methodological limit to keep in mind for every income-adjacent figure below.

## 2. Overall volume trends (full 2025–26 season, all 1,146 members)

| Metric | 2024–25 | 2025–26 | Change | 10-yr change (2016–17→2025–26) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Distinct first-year applicants | 1,495,475 | 1,527,328 | +2% | +70% |
| Total applications | 10,157,068 | 10,786,782 | +6% | +154% |
| Applications per applicant | 6.79 | 7.06 | +4% | +49% |
| Account creators intending to enroll | 2,564,311 | 2,560,533 | **−<1%** | +77% |

**The account-creator decline is the first year-over-year drop since 2016–17** — Common App attributes it partly to the projected decline in U.S. high school graduates starting 2025 (the long-forecast "demographic cliff"). Note the divergence: fewer people are starting the process, but the ones who do are applying to meaningfully more schools than before (7.06 apps/applicant, up 49% over a decade) — total application volume keeps climbing even as the applicant base plateaus.

**Mid-season snapshot (through March 1, 2026, 911 "returning" 5-year members only):** 1,429,747 applicants (+2%), 9,423,621 applications (+5%), 6.59 apps/applicant (+3%).

## 3. Socioeconomic and first-generation trends — the sharpest divergence in this dataset

| Group | YoY growth (2025–26) | Non-group YoY growth | Decade growth |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Fee-waiver-eligible applicants | +6% | 0% (non-eligible) | **+165%** (vs. +39% non-eligible — a >4x gap) |
| Below-median-income ZIP applicants | +8% | +2% (above-median) | **+109%** (vs. +54% above-median) |
| First-generation applicants | +6% | −<1% (continuing-generation) | — |

Lower-income and first-generation applicant volume is growing several times faster than the rest of the pool, every single year and over the full decade — this is the platform's most consistent, unambiguous trend. Common App maintains a dedicated three-part research-brief series specifically on first-gen definitions, trends, and outcomes (linked from its main reports, not independently re-verified in this research pass).

**A real caveat worth carrying into every other file in this project:** first-generation, URM, fee-waiver-eligible, and below-median-income applicants are all **less likely to submit standardized test scores**, though score-reporting is growing faster than non-reporting within every one of these subgroups. This directly bears on how to read the NACAC test-score-importance collapse in `NACAC State of College Admission Data.md` §2 — the applicants driving fastest growth on Common App are also the group most likely to go test-optional, which is at least part of the mechanism behind that national collapse.

## 4. Race/ethnicity trends (domestic applicants only)

| Group | YoY growth (2025–26) | Share of pool, prior year | Share of pool, current |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| URM (Black, Latinx, Am. Indian/AK Native, Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander) | +5% (vs. +3% non-URM) | 36.4% | 36.9% |
| Black/African American | **+8%** (fastest-growing group) | 14.8% | 15.3% |
| Two or More Races | +7% | — | — |
| Asian | +4% | — | — |
| White | — | 44.9% | 44.4% (continuing long-term decline traced to at least 2013–14) |
| Unknown race/ethnicity | — | 3.2% | 3.1% |

Decade growth: URM applicants +137%, non-URM +47%.

**A directly relevant finding for `SFFA Lawsuit Data (Harvard & UNC).md`:** Common App states explicitly, in its own words, that it sees **"no meaningful deviations from pre-existing trends"** in race/ethnicity applicant composition after the June 2023 Supreme Court ruling ending race-conscious admissions — reiterated across both the End-of-Season Report and an earlier dedicated research brief after the 2023–24 season. This is applicant-pool-level data (who applies), not admit-rate data (who gets in) — it doesn't contradict Harvard's own disclosed post-ruling admit shift (documented in the SFFA file), since a stable applicant pool composition is fully compatible with a changed admit-rate composition. The two data points answer different questions and should be read together, not as competing claims.

## 5. Geographic trends

- Slowest growth: Metropolitan ZIP codes (+3%). Faster growth: Small Town (+9%), Rural (+8%), Micropolitan (+7%) — though metros still dominate total volume.
- The **Southwest is the fastest-growing U.S. region** (+9%, nearly 2x the next-fastest region), driven by Texas (+9%, decelerating from +43% the prior year) and Oklahoma.
- **Mississippi was the single fastest-growing state** (+28%).
- Texas has added the most applicants of any state over the full decade (2016–17→2025–26), partly attributed to major public member institutions joining Common App after 2019–20. Florida is now the 4th-highest-volume state.

## 6. International applicant trends — a real, worsening decline

International applicants **declined 10%** in 2025–26, intensifying a decline first flagged in the 2024–25 End-of-Season Report. Asia (the largest international region) fell 11%; Africa fell 17%.

| Country | Change |
|:--|--:|
| India (historically the #2 international country after China) | −15% |
| Ghana | −34% |
| Nigeria | −21% |
| Ethiopia | −29% |
| Kenya (only top-10-volume country to grow) | **+29%** |
| Honduras | +55% |
| Venezuela | +129% |

The Americas region grew overall even as Asia and Africa declined sharply — a real geographic reshuffling of Common App's international pool, not a uniform global decline. (Note: the international decline figure was 9% at the March 1 mid-season snapshot vs. 10% by full-season end — a small but real intra-season shift worth flagging if precision matters.)

## 7. Institutional and selectivity trends

- Applications grew 7% to public member institutions vs. 5% to private (2025–26).
- By selectivity tier, **"Most Selective" institutions (sub-25% admit rate) grew slowest of any tier (+3%)**, versus +5–8% at less-selective tiers — a real, quantified confirmation that application growth is concentrated away from the very top of the selectivity ladder, consistent with the broader "everyone applies everywhere now" dynamic without that growth being evenly distributed.
- 62% of applicants applied to both public and private institutions (up from 60%) — cross-sector application behavior is becoming more common, not less.
- 58.7% of member institutions saw flat or increased application volume; 41.3% saw declines — a genuinely split picture behind the aggregate growth numbers, worth remembering before assuming every Common App member school is seeing rising demand.

## 8. What wasn't found — Early Decision/Early Action

Common App's own End-of-Season Report appendix includes figures on applications by deadline-decision type and applicant deadline-participation behavior going back to 2016–17, but the underlying numbers exist only as **chart images in the PDF**, not as extractable report text — no exact ED/EA figures could be pulled from Common App's own document. Any ED/EA statistics elsewhere in this project (e.g., school-specific figures in `School Admissions Data Reference (CDS + IPEDS).md`) come from individual schools' own CDS filings, not from this platform-level source — flagged here as a real gap rather than guessed at.
