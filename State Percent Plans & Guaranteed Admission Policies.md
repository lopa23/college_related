# State-Level Automatic/Guaranteed Admission Policies ("Percent Plans")

Verified as of 2026-09-23. Companion file to `SFFA Lawsuit Data (Harvard & UNC).md` — these state "percent plans" are the leading race-neutral alternative model that pre-dates and is frequently cited alongside the SFFA litigation.

---

## 1. Texas — Top Ten Percent Rule (Tex. Educ. Code § 51.803)

**Core rule:** Any general academic teaching institution in Texas (i.e., essentially all Texas public 4-year universities) **must** admit a Texas-resident applicant who graduated in the **top 10% of their high school class** (in one of the two school years preceding the year applied for), provided curriculum/deadline requirements are met. Valedictorians qualify regardless of the underlying percentile mechanics. This is a **statewide, all-institutions** guarantee — not limited to one flagship — codified in law, not merely a university system policy.
- Primary source: [Tex. Educ. Code § 51.803, official text via Texas.Public.Law](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._educ._code_section_51.803) (mirrors the state statute; FindLaw copy also available)

**UT Austin special cap (the "capacity constraint" you recalled):** Under **Subsection (a-1)**, added by **S.B. 175 (81st Legislature, 2009)**, UT Austin alone is **not required** to admit automatic-qualifiers beyond **75% of its first-time-resident-undergraduate enrollment capacity** in a given year. The remaining ~25% of seats go through holistic/normal review — which also handles the automatic-qualified students who exceed the 75% cutoff (ranked and admitted down by percentile until the cap is hit, then the rest compete holistically). This cap exists *only* at UT Austin; other Texas public universities admit all qualifying top-10% applicants without a cap.
- Primary source: [TEA correspondence, Oct. 24, 2024, "The University of Texas at Austin Automatic Admission Policy"](https://tea.texas.gov/about-tea/news-and-multimedia/correspondence/taa-letters/taa-2024-10-24-the-university-of-texas-at-austin-automatic-admission-policy)

**Threshold at UT Austin has been repeatedly narrowed** (a *university-specific* percentile, distinct from the statewide 10% floor) as applicant volume grew:
- 2019 entering classes: top 7%
- Then top 6% for several recent cycles
- **New: top 5%**, effective for students entering **fall 2026, and spring 2027** admission cycles, per the TEA letter above, driven by S.B. 175's 75%-capacity mechanism.
- ⚠️ Minor sourcing conflict: some secondary sites (College Kickstart, UsaDemia) describe the "current" UT Austin threshold as 6% while others already describe 5%. The TEA primary source and *Daily Texan* institutional reporting confirm **5% takes effect starting with fall 2026 admission** — treat 6% as the outgoing threshold, 5% as the incoming one.
- Secondary corroboration: [The Daily Texan, Nov. 21, 2024, "University lowers automatic admissions threshold to 5%"](https://thedailytexan.com/2024/11/21/university-lowers-automatic-admissions-threshold-to-5-what-this-means-for-future-applicants/)

**Recent legislative change (non-traditional students):** **H.B. 3041 (2025)** amended § 51.803 to let non-traditional secondary-education applicants (e.g., homeschool/no class rank) qualify via a high test-score alternative (SAT ≥1570 or ACT 36) for Spring 2027–Spring 2028 cycles, since they lack a class rank to measure against.

**Origin:** Enacted as **House Bill 588**, signed by Gov. George W. Bush, **May 20, 1997**, effective for the 1998 admission cycle — a direct, deliberate **race-neutral response to *Hopwood v. Texas*** (5th Cir. 1996), which barred UT Austin Law School (and by extension, Texas public higher ed in the 5th Circuit) from considering race in admissions. The rule leveraged persistent school-level segregation/inequality (high-achieving students at predominantly-minority high schools) to reconstitute racial diversity without a racial classification.
- [Hopwood v. Texas, Wikipedia (summary of the case and its holding)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopwood_v._Texas) — use only as a pointer; verify against the 5th Circuit opinion (78 F.3d 932) if quoting the holding directly.
- [Texas House Bill 588, Wikipedia — legislative history summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_House_Bill_588)

**Interaction with holistic review:** Non-top-10%-qualifying applicants (and, at UT Austin, top-10% applicants beyond the 75% cap) go through the university's normal holistic process, which under Texas law may legally *not* consider race directly post-*Hopwood*/post-*SFFA*, but does weigh GPA, rigor, essays, and other factors.

## 2. California — Eligibility in the Local Context (ELC) + Statewide Index

The UC system runs **two distinct, parallel** percent-based eligibility pathways; neither is a state *law* — both are **University of California systemwide admission policy** (Board of Regents / UC Office of the President), not statute.

### 2a. Eligibility in the Local Context (ELC) — school-specific
- Guarantees consideration to California-resident students in the **top 9% of their own individual high school's class** (not statewide), at UC-participating high schools.
- GPA is calculated from **UC-approved ("A–G") coursework completed in 10th and 11th grade only**, benchmarked against each school's own top-15%-of-junior-class transcript data to set a school-specific cutoff GPA; **minimum 3.0 GPA** and specific A–G course-pattern completion required.
- Threshold was **expanded from top 4% to top 9%** starting with the **2012 admission cycle**.
- **What it guarantees:** ELC does **not** guarantee admission to an applicant's first-choice UC campus. It guarantees that if the student applies to and is not admitted to any UC campus they chose, they will be **offered a space at some other UC campus with available space** — a systemwide floor, not a campus-specific right.
- Primary source: [UC Admissions — "Local guarantee (ELC)," official UC Admissions site](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/admission-requirements/first-year-requirements/california-residents/local-guarantee-elc.html)
- Primary source: [UCOP — "Eligibility in the Local Context (ELC) Program"](https://www.ucop.edu/enrollment-services/programs-and-initiatives/eligibility-local-context.html)

### 2b. Eligibility in the Statewide Context (Statewide Index/Guarantee)
- Parallel pathway identifying the **top 9% of California high school graduates statewide** (as opposed to ELC's per-school 9%), using a **Statewide Index** combining A–G course count and UC GPA.
- **Revised effective fall 2022** to drop standardized test scores from the index, since UC eliminated SAT/ACT consideration systemwide.
- Same guarantee scope as ELC: **space at some UC campus, not first choice.**
- Primary source: [UC Admissions — "Statewide guarantee"](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/admission-requirements/first-year-requirements/california-residents/statewide-guarantee/) and its [Admissions Index instructions subpage](https://admission.universityofcalifornia.edu/admission-requirements/first-year-requirements/california-residents/statewide-guarantee/admissions-index-instructions.html)

**Relationship between the two:** A student can qualify via either or both; they're independent 9% calculations (own-high-school percentile vs. statewide percentile) that both resolve to the same kind of guarantee — a UC seat somewhere, contingent on space and minimum eligibility, never a specific campus. Neither pathway is legally mandated the way Texas's is; UC could alter or discontinue either by Regental/systemwide policy action without new state legislation.

**Origin context:** ELC was adopted in 2001 (effective 2001-02 admission cycle) following **Proposition 209 (1996)**, California's voter-approved ban on public-sector affirmative action (which predates and is broader than the later *SFFA* litigation), as UC's own race-neutral mechanism to preserve access for strong students at lower-resourced/high-minority high schools. (Then-Governor Gray Davis's original 1999 proposal floated a 4% threshold, later expanded to 9% in 2012 — confirmed via multiple secondary sources; recommend independently checking a UC Regents policy document if you need a primary citation for this specific historical figure.)

## 3. Florida — Talented Twenty Program

- **Legal basis:** Not a state statute directly (unlike Texas) — established by **Executive Order 99-281 ("One Florida Initiative")**, issued by Gov. Jeb Bush in **1999**, and implemented via **State University System Board of Governors Rule 6.002(2)(c), F.A.C.**, administered by the Florida Department of Education.
- **Current mechanics:** Guarantees a Florida public-high-school graduate who (a) earns a standard diploma, (b) ranks in the **top 20% of their graduating class** as of 7th-semester grades, and (c) completes required college-prep coursework and testing, **admission to one of Florida's twelve State University System (SUS) institutions** — but **not to the institution of the student's choice**. There is no student application; schools/districts identify and certify eligible seniors automatically.
- **Status confirmed active for the 2024–25 school year** per FLDOE's own program page and welcome-letter PDF (dated for the 2024-25 cycle).
- Primary source: [FLDOE — "Talented Twenty Program," official program page](https://www.fldoe.org/schools/family-community/activities-programs/pre-collegiate/talented-twenty-program/)
- Note: the FLDOE "Talented Twenty Program Welcome" PDF for 2024–25 exists at `cdn.fldoe.org` but returned binary/unreadable content on automated fetch in this research pass; verify directly at the FLDOE program page above, which links to it, if you need the PDF itself.
- Ancillary benefit: Talented Twenty designees receive **priority consideration for Florida Student Assistance Grant (FSAG)**, a need-based grant — separate from and not to be confused with the merit-based **Bright Futures Scholarship** (Fla. Stat. § 1009.531), which is a different program with its own GPA/test-score tiers and is **not** an admission guarantee.
- **Origin/purpose:** Created as the admissions component of the One Florida Initiative, which **simultaneously banned race-conscious affirmative action** in Florida public university admissions, government hiring, and state contracting. It was explicitly designed — with input from Black Republican SUS Chancellor Adam Herbert and civil-rights figure T. Willard Fair — as the race-neutral substitute intended to sustain minority enrollment after affirmative action was eliminated by executive order, paralleling Texas's *Hopwood*-driven rationale but arising from a governor's executive action rather than a court ruling.
- Sources: [Ballotpedia — "Affirmative action in Florida"](https://ballotpedia.org/Affirmative_action_in_Florida); [Washington Post, Jan. 7, 2016, "Jeb Bush got his way on affirmative action in Florida. Then he got a mess."](https://www.washingtonpost.com/sf/national/2016/01/07/decidersbush/)

## 4. Other states — search results

A broad search for "percent plan" state admission laws surfaced **no other currently-active state-law-level percent plans** comparable in scope to Texas's. Secondary sources (City Journal, Manhattan Institute) that specifically survey "percent plans" as a policy category consistently discuss only **Texas, California, and Florida** as the three states that adopted this model in the late 1990s/early 2000s in response to affirmative-action bans (Texas via *Hopwood*, California via Prop 209, Florida via One Florida). No results indicated an active percent-plan law in New Mexico, Nevada, Illinois, or elsewhere — those states use conventional or separate guaranteed-admission-by-GPA/test-score index systems (e.g., some state flagships offer automatic admission by GPA+test thresholds, which is a related but distinct mechanism from a *class-rank percentile* plan and wasn't in scope here). If your project needs those systems characterized too, that's a separate research pass — flagging rather than asserting they don't exist, since this was a web-search pass, not an exhaustive 50-state statutory review.
- [City Journal — "'Percent Plans' Undermine Meritocracy in Higher Education"](https://www.city-journal.org/article/state-percent-plans-college-admissions-race-affirmative-action)
- [Manhattan Institute — "Why 'Percent Plans' for University Admissions Aren't a Colorblind Success"](https://manhattan.institute/article/why-percent-plans-for-university-admissions-are-not-a-colorblind-success)
