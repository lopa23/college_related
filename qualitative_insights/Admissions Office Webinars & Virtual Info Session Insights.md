# Admissions Office Webinars & Virtual Info Session Insights

> **What this file is:** A themed synthesis of **webinars and virtual info sessions hosted directly by university admissions offices' own YouTube channels or official webinar platforms** — the video-content counterpart to `MIT Admissions Blog Insights & Process Guide.md` and `Admissions Office Blogs & Podcasts Insights.md`. Coverage spans MIT, Stanford, Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Penn, Duke, Northwestern, Johns Hopkins, Georgia Tech, UC Berkeley, and Michigan. Full transcripts were generally **not accessible** (most official channels don't publish captions/transcripts as readable text, and several institutional "on-demand resource" pages are gated behind applicant-portal logins, returning 403 errors to direct access). All content below is therefore based on **video titles, channel descriptions, on-screen chapter markers, and official landing-page text**, paraphrased in my own words — per copyright practice, at most one short (<15-word) direct quote is used per source, always attributed.

> **What this file is NOT:** A transcript archive. Where a finding is backed only by a title/short description rather than substantive on-screen content, it is explicitly flagged **[TITLE-ONLY]**. Several schools' primary webinar archives are gated (Columbia's "On-Demand Resources," Princeton's "Preview Online," Michigan's recorded-session portal) — I flag this honestly rather than guessing at their content.

## 1. Yale — the strongest webinar content found in this research

Yale Undergraduate Admissions runs the most substantive, verifiably-official webinar presence of any school researched here, all hosted on its own YouTube channel (not gated):

- **"Yale Admissions Virtual Information Session"** (~1 hour, ~5.4K views, uploaded ~2022): co-hosted by **admissions officer "Mark"** and a **current Yale senior, "Emma"** — covers undergraduate academic programs, student life, financial aid, and the admissions process together, pairing an official and a peer voice in one session, mirroring the officer/student pairing pattern already documented in MIT's blog file. **[TITLE + description confidence — full transcript not accessed]**
- **"Yale Affordability and Admissions Session"** (24:14, ~6.8K views, Aug 2022): solo-hosted by the same officer, Mark, opening with a stated chapter titled "Affordable for Everyone: Yale's Approach to Need-Based Aid" before moving into general admissions — the pairing of "affordability" and "admissions" in one session (rather than a separate financial-aid-only webinar) suggests Yale deliberately frames cost as inseparable from the admissions conversation, not an afterthought. **[TITLE + chapter-marker confidence]**
- **"Virtual Admissions Webinar on the Eli Whitney Students Program"** (1:12:50, ~1 year old) and **"Eli Whitney Students Program Application and Financial Aid Workshop"** (1:04:57, ~7 months old): Yale maintains dedicated, recurring webinar coverage for its Eli Whitney Program specifically — Yale's nontraditional/returning-student admissions track — distinct from its standard first-year process, run as its own ongoing series rather than a one-off. **[TITLE-ONLY]**

Yale's officer-hosted podcast, "Inside the Yale Admissions Office" (Mark Dunn & Hannah Mendlowitz), is already documented via full transcripts in the companion file `Admissions Office Blogs & Podcasts Insights.md` §3 — its "Financial Aid 101" episode (Dec 2021) covers Yale's four aid tenets in more depth than the video webinars above; treat that podcast file as the higher-confidence source for Yale financial aid mechanics, this section as confirming Yale runs the same content through a second, video-webinar channel.

## 2. Georgia Tech — a real, recurring annual webinar plus a fresh short-form series

GTAdmission's YouTube channel (100 videos) shows two genuinely distinct, current-cycle content types:

- **"2026-2027 Financial Aid Webinar"** (47:11, 8.8K views, uploaded ~10 months ago): a full-length, single annual webinar re-uploaded each cycle (prior-year versions like "2025-2026 Financial Aid Webinar" also exist) — but the video carries **no written description**, so beyond confirming its existence, length, and recurring annual cadence, no topic-level content could be verified. **[TITLE-ONLY — honest limitation]**
- **"First-Year Application" series** — four short videos (~3–5 min each) uploaded roughly one month before this research: *Essays*, *Academic Review*, *Standardized Testing*, and *Contribution to Community*, plus a parallel *International First-Year Admission* video. This fresh, current-cycle series maps directly onto GT's own blog's stated evaluation themes (see `Admissions Office Blogs & Podcasts Insights.md` §1 — committee-based review, "mission drives admission") — the titles independently confirm the same four-part structure the blog describes in prose. **[TITLE-ONLY, corroborates existing blog content]**
- Georgia Tech also runs narrowly-scoped explainers — "Understanding Your Financial Aid Offer," "Waitlist Overview and Next Steps," "Complete your FAFSA: It's not too Late!" — timed to specific points in the admissions calendar rather than general-purpose webinars.

## 3. Duke — one clearly documented officer-led info session

**"Information Session featuring Admissions Officer Nasya Bernard-Lucien '23"** (Duke Undergraduate Admissions channel, uploaded Feb 2026): the clearest single example found of a named-officer general info session with enough structure to describe confidently. On-screen chapter markers show the session moves through: introduction → location and campus life → student community and housing → facilities and student life → academic overview → **Trinity College of Arts and Sciences** → **Pratt School of Engineering** → academic support resources. The chapter structure itself (splitting "academic overview" into Duke's two undergraduate schools by name) is a useful, specific confirmation that Duke's general info sessions explicitly walk applicants through its two-school academic structure rather than treating Duke as a single undifferentiated college. **[Chapter-marker confidence — no transcript pulled]**

## 4. Johns Hopkins — a substantive short-video "Admissions Tips" library, not classic webinars

Johns Hopkins's admissions YouTube channel (128 videos) runs a distinct, well-populated **"Admissions Tips"** series of short (2–4 minute) official videos rather than long-form live webinars: *How We Review Applications* (21K views — the series' clear standout), *Personal Statement*, *Activities List*, *Additional Information Section*, *Community Disruption Supplement* (a Hopkins-specific supplemental-essay prompt), *When to Apply to Hopkins*, *Affording Hopkins*, plus separate tracks for *Transfer Admissions* and *Undergraduate Admissions for Veterans*. Longer-form entries exist alongside these: *"How to Apply for Financial Aid at Hopkins"* (7:11, 5.1K views) and *"Understanding College Costs & Affordability"* (9:48, 4.6K views), plus a process-adjacent explainer, *"School Profile & Counselor Recommendation Letters in Highly Selective Admissions"* (4:06, 4.1K views) — notable because it signals Hopkins publicly coaches counselors/applicants on how school-profile context and rec letters are read, a topic usually only discussed anecdotally (e.g., the AMA file's caseload-context claims). **All entries here are [TITLE-ONLY]** — video descriptions were brief or absent; the topic list itself is the main verifiable finding, not detailed content.

## 5. Princeton — a well-specified live session format, but panel archive is gated

Princeton's own admission-office landing page (not a third party) specifies its **Virtual Information Session** format precisely: a **30-minute presentation followed by 15 minutes of live Q&A**, covering four fixed topics — university overview, the admission process, the financial aid program, and student experience — with English closed captioning available. Notably, the page states directly, close-paraphrased: Princeton does **not** track session attendance for evaluating an applicant's demonstrated interest, and multiple household members may watch under one registration — directly consistent with Yale's own no-demonstrated-interest-tracking claim documented in the companion podcast file, now a second, independent confirmation from a different Ivy. Princeton's richer content — the seasonal "Preview Online" portal, which includes a stated "Ask Us Anything" panel among faculty/student-life/campus-resource panels — is **gated behind admitted-applicant portal credentials** and could not be accessed directly; only its existence and topic list (from the portal's own public description) could be confirmed. **[Landing-page confidence for the general session; TITLE-ONLY for Preview Online panels]**

## 6. UC Berkeley — Personal Insight Question workshop, one primary + one secondary source

Berkeley's "UC Berkeley Unlocked" programming included a dated **Personal Insight Question (PIQ) Workshop** (held July 30, 2026, per the admissions office's own events listing) described as covering what reviewers look for, prompt selection, storytelling technique, and common mistakes — directly from Berkeley's own events page. **[Landing-page confidence]** Separately, a third-party outlet (Expert Admissions, not Berkeley-authored) hosted its own webinar featuring **Jake Jerzy, Senior Associate Director of Undergraduate Admissions at UC Berkeley**, discussing the UC application and Berkeley's holistic review process — flagged as **secondary corroboration, not primary institutional testimony**, since Expert Admissions (an independent consultancy) organized and hosts that recording, not Berkeley itself.

## 7. Thin or inaccessible: MIT, Stanford, Harvard, Columbia, Penn, Northwestern, Michigan

Being honest about weak findings, as the task requires:

- **MIT**: `mitadmissions.org/online-info-sessions` is purely a registration gateway into a live Zoom-based portal (`apply.mitadmissions.org/portal/virtualmit`) — no public YouTube recordings or session recaps were found for MIT's undergraduate admissions webinars specifically (distinct from MIT Sloan/graduate-program webinars, which are separate and plentiful). The page itself discloses no topics, hosts, or frequency. **[Landing-page only, and a thin one]**
- **Stanford**: the verified official undergraduate channel, `@engagestanford`, has only 18 total videos; aside from three recent **transfer-applicant-specific** sessions ("Transfer Session Recording 2026," two "Transfer Resources 101" uploads), its remaining content is 6–13-year-old campus-tour/feature videos, including a 9-year-old "Interview with Dean Shaw" (a former dean, now outdated). **There is no evident current freshman-applicant webinar series** on Stanford's own channel — a genuine, notable gap for a school this selective. **[Channel-survey confidence — negative finding]**
- **Harvard**: `@HarvardCollegeAdmissions` is large (222 videos, 60.6K subscribers) but overwhelmingly produced short-form lifestyle content (dorm tours, "day in the life," acai-bowl-tasting videos) rather than panel webinars. Two relevant exceptions: **"Understanding the Harvard Admissions Process"** (7:20, uploaded ~2 weeks before this research), described as organized around common prospective-family questions in three parts, and **"Understanding Harvard College Financial Aid"** (2:26) plus **"What Financial Aid Is Like at Harvard"** (4:36) — all short, produced FAQ-format videos, not live Q&A webinars. **[TITLE + brief-description confidence]**
- **Columbia**: `Columbia Undergraduate Admissions` (445 videos) is dominated (nearly every recent upload) by a long-running student "Day in the Life" vlog series; Columbia's actual webinar archive lives behind a gated "On-Demand Resources" page and the "Countdown to Columbia" summer series page, both of which returned access errors (403) during this research — genuinely inaccessible, not just unsummarized.
- **Penn**: the specific "Ask Admissions: Application Overview and FAQs" video found is run by **Penn Engineering's graduate admissions office**, not Penn's undergraduate College admissions office — an important distinction not to conflate; Penn's undergraduate-specific webinar content could not be independently verified beyond the existence of a `Penn Admissions` YouTube channel and a `key.admissions.upenn.edu/portal/virtual-events` portal.
- **Northwestern**: publishes a clear recurring **schedule** of school-specific "Academic Information Sessions" (Bienen School of Music, School of Communication, McCormick Engineering, Medill Journalism, Weinberg Arts & Sciences, SESP) at fixed weekly times, both on-campus and online — but the page does not name hosts or confirm whether sessions are recorded for later on-demand viewing. **[Landing-page confidence, format only]**
- **Michigan**: `admissions.umich.edu`'s virtual-resources and school-and-college-info-session pages both returned 403 errors during direct access; YouTube search results confirm recorded info sessions exist (e.g., a June 2023 general info session, an October 2023 first-year session) but content detail could not be verified beyond titles.

## 8. Cross-source patterns worth trusting

- **Officer-plus-student co-hosting** recurs independently at Yale (Mark + Emma) and is the implied format at Duke (a single recent alum admissions officer) and Northwestern (admission director + current student, per its own page description) — a consistent institutional format choice across unrelated schools, not one school's idiosyncrasy.
- **No demonstrated-interest tracking, stated on the record**, now appears independently from **Princeton's own admission-office webpage** and **Yale's podcast** (documented in the companion file) — two Ivies making the same claim through two different official channels.
- **Short "tips" video libraries (2–5 min each) rather than long panel webinars** is the dominant format at Johns Hopkins and increasingly at Georgia Tech (its new "First-Year Application" series) — suggesting a shift among some offices toward bite-sized, searchable video FAQs over hour-long live sessions, versus Yale's continued investment in full-length sessions.
- **Financial aid gets its own dedicated webinar/video treatment almost everywhere** (Yale, Georgia Tech, Johns Hopkins, Harvard, Berkeley) even when general admissions webinar content is thin — financial aid appears to be the one topic virtually every office treats as warranting standalone video content regardless of how developed its broader webinar presence is.

## 9. Reliability caveats

1. **No full transcripts were obtained for any video in this file.** YouTube does not expose readable transcript/caption text to standard web-fetch tools without interactive, per-video UI actions; where a browser tool was used to inspect a video page directly, only the title, channel, view count, upload date, and (where present) the short written description or on-screen chapter markers were captured — never the spoken content itself. Every claim above is scoped to that limitation and flagged accordingly.
2. **Several institutional archives are access-gated** (Columbia's On-Demand Resources, Princeton's Preview Online, Michigan's session-recording pages) and returned HTTP 403 errors rather than content — these are reported as inaccessible, not summarized from guesswork.
3. **Channel size and video count are not proxies for webinar depth** — Harvard (222 videos) and Columbia (445 videos) are the two largest channels surveyed but have the *least* actual webinar-panel content, being dominated instead by short lifestyle/vlog series; Yale (a much smaller channel) had the most substantive long-form officer-led session content found in this entire research pass.
4. Video upload dates given as "~X years/months ago" reflect YouTube's own relative-date display at time of research (September 2026) and were not independently converted to exact calendar dates unless a specific date was separately confirmed (e.g., Yale's Aug 25, 2022 upload, Duke's Feb 2, 2026 upload).
